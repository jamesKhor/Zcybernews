## Executive Summary

A threat actor tracked as BUGSTER used a jailbroken AI assistant to plan and execute a string of cybercrimes against targets in India, including a confirmed intrusion into a university that exposed more than 5,200 records containing student personally identifiable information. Unit 42, which published its findings on October 21, said the actor relied on the Hermes Agent framework configured with DeepSeek V4 as its reasoning engine and the Cursor IDE as a secondary tool, both with safety controls disabled.

The investigation is notable less for the sophistication of the intrusion than for what it says about the lowering skill floor. Unit 42 assessed that the actor used basic tooling and leaned on AI-generated scripts and reconnaissance reports, suggesting they lacked the skills these activities would have required before readily available AI tools. Despite what the researchers described as "apparent bumbling," the actor mapped multiple organizations' attack surfaces and recovered thousands of PII records.

## Technical Analysis

Unit 42 reconstructed the actor's operational environment and found the AI assistant weaponized across several distinct crime types: credential theft and data exfiltration, VoIP telecommunications fraud, Wi-Fi password cracking, cryptocurrency reconnaissance, and command-and-control operations.

The most fully documented campaign targeted an Indian university's online education platform and progressed through four stages. The actor first enumerated 12 subdomains, including the main WordPress site, a learning management system, a student portal, and an administration dashboard. A second stage abused the WordPress REST API to extract 13 active user IDs with real names via post author fields and author sitemaps — a technique that bypassed the Cloudflare Web Application Firewall blocking direct access to user endpoints. The actor then generated a brute-force tool seeded with 117 enumerated usernames and 28 common passwords; the tool flagged one WordPress login as valid.

Unit 42 said the enumeration reports carried structured formatting — executive summaries, severity ratings, systematic endpoint categorization — consistent with AI-generated output. The actor also achieved a confirmed intrusion against a second university, exfiltrating a database of more than 5,200 records including student PII.

Beyond the university intrusions, the actor built specialized AI bot profiles to acquire VoIP fraud expertise through conversation and maintained automated pipelines for Wi-Fi password cracking and cryptocurrency whale reconnaissance, meaning the identification of large cryptocurrency holders. Unit 42 noted that the Wi-Fi cracking capability requires close physical proximity to the target, indicating the actor had local access to the targeted locations.

One assistant, per Unit 42, "helpfully" generated a document containing a "NEXT EXPLOIT OPPORTUNITIES" section outlining planned follow-up attacks — the phrase Unit 42 adopted for its report title.

The researchers flagged a broader trend: multiple, unrelated actors are independently adopting the same Hermes plus DeepSeek combination. Unit 42 assessed a key driver is low safety friction at low cost — DeepSeek models are inexpensive, easy to access, and readily jailbroken with a simple system-prompt override.

## Tactics, Techniques & Procedures

The campaign's sequencing is instructive. Reconnaissance and enumeration preceded tool generation, and the brute-force script was built from the enumeration output rather than pulled from a public toolkit — a workflow consistent with an AI assistant generating bespoke tooling on request. The REST API enumeration that bypassed Cloudflare is a known WordPress weakness, but the actor's use of post author fields and author sitemaps to harvest real names suggests the assistant supplied the technique rather than the actor discovering it independently.

Unit 42's TTP mapping spans active scanning, victim network information gathering, password guessing, exfiltration over web service, capability development, and account establishment for the AI bot profiles. The Wi-Fi cracking and cryptocurrency reconnaissance pipelines indicate the actor was diversifying monetization rather than pursuing a single objective.

## Threat Actor Context

BUGSTER is not a named APT or established ransomware affiliate. Unit 42 characterized the actor as lacking the skills these activities would have required before the advent of readily available AI tools, and described the operation as marked by "apparent bumbling" despite its impact. The researchers did not attribute the actor to a state or known criminal group. The more significant attribution is toolchain-level: Unit 42 said multiple unrelated actors are converging on Hermes Agent with DeepSeek V4, which it described as an increasingly prevalent toolchain of choice for cybercriminals adopting AI-enabled capabilities.

## Mitigations & Recommendations

Unit 42's disclosure centers on detection and exposure reduction rather than a patchable flaw. Defenders should treat AI-generated reconnaissance as a signal: structured enumeration reports and rapid, methodical subdomain and REST API probing against WordPress estates are worth alerting on even when individual requests look benign. The Cloudflare WAF bypass via author sitemaps and post author fields is a concrete gap — organizations running WordPress should restrict or monitor REST API user enumeration rather than relying on WAF rules that block only direct user endpoints.

Because the Wi-Fi cracking capability required local proximity, physical and wireless security reviews at sensitive sites remain relevant. Unit 42 also recommended its AI Security Assessment and Frontier AI Defense services for organizations assessing AI-enabled risk, and said Palo Alto Networks customers are covered through Advanced URL Filtering, Advanced IP Defense, Cortex XDR and XSIAM, Cortex Agentic Endpoint Security, Cortex Cloud, and the Idira identity and secrets management products.
