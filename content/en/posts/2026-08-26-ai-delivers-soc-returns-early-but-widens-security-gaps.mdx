## Executive Summary

A new survey of 611 North American security decision-makers, commissioned by SentinelOne and conducted by 451 Research, shows that early-stage AI is already delivering measurable returns in security operations — even before most organizations reach advanced AI maturity. 99% of respondents report improvements in incident response and remediation, while 96% still operate AI at the most basic maturity levels. However, the same report flags a critical tension: organizations are deploying AI faster than they are securing it, creating new attack surface in agents, data pipelines, and model endpoints.

## Technical Analysis

The survey, now in its second year, challenges the assumption that AI returns only arrive after full maturity. SentinelOne's report indicates that even Level 1 (basic monitoring, triage specialist) and Level 2 (senior triage analyst, basic incident responder) AI implementations are yielding results. This suggests that the infrastructure foundation, not AI maturity, is the primary driver of early value.

Platformization is a key enabler. 82% of organizations now describe themselves as platform-oriented, a 13-point jump year-over-year, with 94% expecting to reach that state within three years. The data shows that EDR, SIEM, and CNAPP — often deployed as standalone tools — are also the most common anchors for integrated platforms. The shift is not about replacing tools but about creating a common data layer that enables coordinated AI decision-making.

The report explicitly ties data infrastructure to AI success. The top-cited benefit of investing in a data lake for SecOps is supporting AI-driven workloads and agents. Organizations that built this foundation early have cleared the barrier that stalls others, and the gap is widening.

Beyond detection metrics, the survey finds rising job satisfaction among analysts as AI handles repetitive triage. The role is shifting toward investigation and threat hunting, which carries real dollar value in a market where SOC analyst turnover is a persistent cost.

## Mitigations & Recommendations

The report's most urgent warning is the security gap: AI infrastructure is being deployed faster than it is secured. An AI agent with misconfigured access or an unmonitored data pipeline is an exposure. Defenders should treat AI systems — including agents, data pipelines, and model endpoints — as part of the attack surface and apply the same monitoring, access control, and governance as any other critical asset. Organizations without a clear governance posture are accepting risk that may not be priced into their AI investment case. The platform that runs the autonomous SOC and the platform that secures it are increasingly the same platform.
