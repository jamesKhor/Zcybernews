## Executive Summary

Legacy operational technology (OT) systems in manufacturing and critical infrastructure environments are increasingly exposed to cyber threats as decades-old industrial equipment becomes connected to corporate IT networks. According to the SANS Institute, nearly 60% of OT attacks across various industries originate from compromises in corporate IT environments. The same survey found that 22% of organizations in essential industries reported a cybersecurity incident over the past year, with 40% of those events causing operational disruption and nearly 20% requiring more than a month to remediate.

## Technical Analysis

Many manufacturing plants rely on OT systems designed for stability and uptime, often running the same physical processes for years without modification. These systems were built when industrial protocols lacked security features such as authentication, encryption, or logging — a design choice that posed little risk when machines were air-gapped from enterprise networks. The convergence of IT and OT, driven by digitization and Industry 4.0 initiatives, has fundamentally changed that risk equation.

Industrial control systems (ICS) now frequently connect to networks they were never designed for, inheriting vulnerabilities from weak authentication, insecure defaults, and update processes that require costly production downtime. The institutional trust earned by long-running, reliable equipment can create a security blind spot, as the logic of "leave well enough alone" persists despite the shifting threat landscape.

ESET's analysis, published on WeLiveSecurity, notes that two or three decades ago, internet-borne attacks were not a concern for most manufacturing environments. The threat either did not exist or was limited to nation-state targets. Today, however, the same industrial protocols that lack built-in security are exposed to adversaries who can pivot from compromised corporate IT systems into OT networks.

## Mitigations & Recommendations

Defenders should prioritize network segmentation between IT and OT environments, implementing strict access controls and monitoring for lateral movement. For legacy systems that cannot be patched or upgraded, compensating controls such as application allowlisting, network intrusion detection tailored to industrial protocols, and out-of-band management should be deployed. Organizations should also conduct regular OT asset inventories and vulnerability assessments, recognizing that the old bargain — don't touch it if it works — has become a liability. Incident response plans must account for the operational disruption potential and extended remediation timelines highlighted by the SANS data.
