## Executive Summary

Enterprise security operations centers (SOCs) are systematically ignoring low-severity and informational security alerts, leading to an average of one missed genuine threat per week per organization. This finding comes from a report analyzing over 25 million alerts across live enterprise environments, including 10 million monitored endpoints and workloads, according to a source familiar with the research who spoke with The Hacker News. The dataset reveals that defenders have institutionalized the practice of not investigating alerts classified as low or informational severity, creating a blind spot that attackers can exploit.

## Technical Analysis

The report, which examined alert data from multiple enterprise deployments, found that the volume of low-severity alerts — often benign or false positives — has trained analysts to triage them out of their workflow entirely. The analysis indicates that approximately 1 in every 1,000 low-severity alerts represents a genuine security event that would warrant escalation if reviewed. At the observed alert volumes, this translates to roughly one missed threat per organization per week.

A key finding is that the problem is not solely about alert volume but about classification accuracy. Many detection rules generate informational events that lack sufficient context for triage, forcing analysts to either investigate blindly or ignore them. The report's authors argue that the industry's focus on reducing false positives has inadvertently created a class of "true positives that look like false positives" — low-signal events that are genuine but indistinguishable from noise without additional enrichment.

The dataset spans diverse sectors including finance, healthcare, and technology, suggesting the pattern is widespread rather than confined to specific verticals. The source noted that the 10 million monitored assets represent a cross-section of enterprise environments, from cloud workloads to on-premises infrastructure.

## Mitigations & Recommendations

Defenders should implement automated enrichment pipelines that correlate low-severity alerts with external threat intelligence, user behavior analytics, and asset criticality before presenting them to human analysts. The report recommends tuning detection rules to suppress known benign patterns rather than entire severity tiers. Organizations should also conduct periodic audits of ignored alert categories to identify recurring genuine events that are being missed. For SOCs with sufficient resources, establishing a dedicated "low-severity review" shift — even one hour per day — can recover the missed threats without overwhelming analysts.
