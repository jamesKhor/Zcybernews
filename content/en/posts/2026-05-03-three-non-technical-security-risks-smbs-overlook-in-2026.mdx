## Executive Summary

Small businesses continue to overlook three fundamental, non-technical security risks that require no specialized cybersecurity expertise to address, according to a May 2026 advisory from Malwarebytes Labs. The risks — weak password practices, unpatched software, and untrained employees — account for a disproportionate share of breaches affecting SMBs, yet each can be mitigated with low-cost, administrative changes.

## Technical Analysis

Malwarebytes identifies the first risk as poor credential hygiene. Many small businesses still rely on reused or weak passwords across critical accounts, with no multi-factor authentication (MFA) enforced. The second risk is delayed or absent software patching. Small operations often lack dedicated IT staff to track and apply updates, leaving known vulnerabilities exploitable for months. The third risk is the absence of basic security awareness training. Employees at SMBs frequently fall for phishing emails or social engineering tactics because they have never been taught to recognize them.

Notably, all three risks are administrative rather than technical in nature. Malwarebytes emphasizes that fixing them does not require purchasing new tools or hiring specialists — only consistent policy enforcement and minimal employee education.

## Mitigations & Recommendations

Malwarebytes recommends that small business owners implement a password manager for all accounts, enable automatic updates on operating systems and commonly used software, and conduct a brief annual security training session for all staff. These steps, the firm notes, address the most common entry points for attackers targeting SMBs without requiring a dedicated cybersecurity budget.
