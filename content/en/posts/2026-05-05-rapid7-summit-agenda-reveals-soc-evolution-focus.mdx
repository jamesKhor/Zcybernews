## Executive Summary

Rapid7 published the full agenda for its 2026 Global Cybersecurity Summit, scheduled for May 12–13, 2026. The program emphasizes a shift from reactive threat response toward earlier detection, exposure management, and AI-assisted decision-making. The summit is organized into two days: the first focuses on how threats evolve and how teams respond, while the second splits into separate tracks for security leaders and practitioners.

## Technical Analysis

Day 1 opens with a keynote titled "Defense Starts Earlier Than You Think," featuring Rapid7's Brian Castagna and IDC Research Vice President Craig Robinson. The session argues that organizational complexity has become the primary barrier to effective security operations, according to the blog post by Emma Burdett.

A panel titled "The Reality of Running a SOC in 2026" includes Raj Samani, Rachel Tobac (CEO of SocialProof Security), and Graham Cluley. The discussion is expected to cover how attacks originate from identity misuse and cloud misconfigurations, and why defenders struggle to keep pace as attacks evolve.

The agenda includes a session on exposure management titled "Beyond the Vulnerability List," which examines how organizations are moving from static vulnerability tracking to using exposure as an early signal for detection and response. Another session, "Using Red Teaming to Power Preemptive MDR," covers continuous adversary testing to validate detection coverage before incidents occur.

Day 2 splits into two tracks. The leader track includes sessions on the evolving CISO role, aligning exposure data with business risk, and measuring MDR effectiveness through outcome-based metrics. The practitioner track covers detection engineering as code, cloud attack escalation scenarios, and incident response workflows using open-source tools.

Notable speakers include Debby Briggs (CISO, Netscout Systems), Raheem Daya (CTO, Target RWE), Jonathan Chow (CISO, Genesys), and Tony Arnold (CISO, TSB Bank). The closing keynote on Day 1 features former Special Forces operator Jason Fox, discussing preparation and decision-making under pressure.

## Mitigations & Recommendations

While this is a conference agenda rather than a threat advisory, security teams can use the summit's themes to evaluate their own operations. Organizations should assess whether their SOC is prioritizing exposure management over reactive patching, and whether detection engineering workflows incorporate adversary-informed testing. Teams may also consider evaluating MDR providers based on outcome metrics rather than alert volume.
