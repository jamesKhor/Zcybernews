## Executive Summary

Rapid7 has gained access to Anthropic's Project Glasswing, including the Claude Mythos Preview model, to explore how frontier AI can support internal defensive security workflows. The partnership, announced by Rapid7 Senior Director of Product Security Wade Woolwine, marks an expansion of Anthropic's initial Project Glasswing cohort to a broader set of organizations. Rapid7 is using the model internally for product security assessments, vulnerability validation, zero-day research, and red-teaming, with a focus on understanding where AI adds defensive value and where human expertise remains essential.

## Technical Analysis

Project Glasswing, launched by Anthropic, provides select organizations with early access to frontier AI capabilities—specifically the Claude Mythos Preview—for evaluating cybersecurity applications. Rapid7's involvement centers on practitioner-led, internal use cases rather than external product integration.

According to Woolwine, Rapid7's initial exploration focuses on four areas:

1. **Product security assessments**: Using Claude Mythos Preview to analyze Rapid7's own code and infrastructure for potential vulnerabilities, weaknesses, or risky patterns that traditional tools might miss. The goal is to reduce risk earlier in the development lifecycle.

2. **Vulnerability validation and exploitation analysis**: Evaluating how the model can help researchers reason across unfamiliar code, validate severity, build safe proof-of-concept exploit paths, and translate findings into remediation guidance—all within authorized environments.

3. **Zero-day research and model evaluation**: Testing the model's performance on vulnerability discovery and exploitation tasks to understand its practical value, limitations, and required safeguards. This includes assessing where frontier AI performs well and where it struggles.

4. **Red-teaming, detection, and response**: Applying the model adversarially to explore attack paths, challenge assumptions, enrich investigations, reduce noise, and support faster decisions when paired with appropriate telemetry and human judgment.

Woolwine emphasized that cybersecurity impact depends on more than model capability. "A model may help identify a potential vulnerability and confirm exploitability, but reducing risk requires deeper operational work: understanding affected systems, mapping business context, prioritizing remediation, validating the fix, and ensuring detection coverage is in place," he wrote.

Rapid7's access comes at a time when attackers are accelerating and attack surfaces are expanding, according to the company. The firm has been tracking Project Glasswing's implications for security leaders, noting that faster discovery is only part of the challenge—the real test is how defenders handle prioritization, remediation, validation, detection, and response.

## Mitigations & Recommendations

Rapid7's approach to frontier AI emphasizes pairing advanced models with trusted security context, expert oversight, and mature operational workflows. Organizations considering similar AI tools should ensure they maintain human-in-the-loop validation for any AI-generated findings, particularly for vulnerability assessment and exploitation analysis. Defenders should treat AI as an augmentation tool rather than a replacement for established security processes, and should conduct their own evaluations of model capabilities and limitations before integrating into production workflows.
